According to the report, there is a significant mismatch between current AI agent capabilities and worker preferences. While AI agents excel in tasks such as data processing and analysis, workers are seeking more human-centric skills like creativity, empathy, and communication. This gap highlights the need for human-agent collaboration in many occupations, rather than relying solely on automation.

The study's findings have significant implications for policymakers, educators, and employers. As AI agents increasingly augment human work, there will be a greater emphasis on interpersonal and organizational skills to ensure workers remain relevant. The report also underscores the importance of capturing worker preferences and AI agent capabilities through tools like the proposed WORKBank Database.

To prepare for this shift, the research paper proposes an auditing framework to assess AI agent capabilities and worker preferences across 104 occupations. This framework could inform policy decisions, guide workforce development initiatives, and empower workers to adapt to changing job requirements. As AI agents continue to transform the US workforce, it is crucial that we prioritize worker voices and develop strategies to support a smooth transition.
